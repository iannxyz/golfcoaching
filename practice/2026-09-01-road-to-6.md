# Practice plan — the road to a 6

Generated by `/coach` from 97 rounds. Supersedes the emphasis in
`2026-09-01-plan.md`, which was aimed at breaking 80. The target changed, so
item 3 changed with it.

## Where the index actually is

Best 8 of the last 20 differentials: **9.2**. The context file says 11.2 — that
is now stale, and it moved fast: 12.3 in May, 11.4 on 1 August, 9.2 today.

To reach **6.0**, the best 8 of any 20 must average a differential of 6.0 —
about **77.3** at Eastlake Blue. Right now that same best 8 averages **81.0**.

**The gap is 3.7 strokes, and it is not one problem.**

## Why doubles alone will not get you there

Fitted on your last 20 rounds:

    score = 76.8 + 2.49 x doubles

Read the intercept. A round with **zero** doubles projects to **76.8** — a 5.5
differential. So the arithmetic says a perfect-discipline round is already a 6
handicap round.

But you need eight of them in twenty, and you have never played one. Best ever
is one double, eight times. Requiring your best 8 rounds to average 0.2 doubles
is not a plan, it is a wish.

So the 3.7 strokes have to come from two places at once: **fewer doubles in the
good rounds, and a lower intercept.** Getting the intercept from 76.8 to ~74.5
makes the target reachable at roughly one double per good round — which you
have already done eight times.

## The three things

### 1. The zero-double round — an event, not an average

You have never had one. That is the single clearest unclaimed milestone in the
log, and it is worth more than any swing change.

Same mantra, unchanged: **first mistake = bogey is now the goal.** The pattern
is clustering, not frequency — four doubles on one nine on 07-31, three
straight on 6-7-8 on 08-30. One bad hole recruits the next.

The drill is on-course, not on the range: after any tee shot in trouble, say
"bogey" out loud before choosing a club, then take the club that guarantees the
next shot is from short grass. Wedging out sideways counts as a win.

**Target: one round with zero doubles inside the next 15.** Then make it repeat.

### 2. Putting — and this is a reversal, so here is the evidence

Across all 92 rounds putting correlates with score at R² = 0.05, and I told you
last time not to work on it. That was right for breaking 80 and wrong for
reaching 6, for a reason worth understanding.

Doubles were swamping the signal. Filter to your recent low-double rounds and
putting becomes the dominant term:

| rounds | putts vs score |
|---|---|
| last 25, all | r = 0.64 |
| last 25, ≤3 doubles | **r = 0.79** |

Look at the clean rounds directly:

| date | score | doubles | GIR | putts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-27 | **76** | 2 | 6 | **26** |
| 2026-08-04 | 81 | 1 | 6 | 32 |
| 2026-08-22 | 82 | 1 | 6 | 29 |
| 2026-05-28 | 79 | 2 | 11 | 34 |

The 76 and the 81 have identical GIR and near-identical doubles. The whole
five-stroke difference is 26 putts versus 32. **Putting is the intercept**, and
the intercept is what a 6 handicap needs.

Caveat, stated plainly: n = 12 for that correlation. It is a strong lead, not an
established fact, and it will confirm or die over the next fifteen rounds.

Work: the L.A.B. is three weeks old and your putts went 26 → 35 → 34 with it.
Lag putting from 30–50 feet, to a three-foot circle, twenty balls. The mantra is
already yours: **past the hole is fine, short is the problem.**

**Target: 30 putts, and under 1.5 three-putts.** You have shown 26, so the
stroke is in there.

### 3. One more club — the free GIR

Approaches missed **short 38 times, long 10** over the last ten rounds. Nearly
four to one. That is not a strike problem, it is a club-selection problem, and
it is the cheapest GIR available to you.

Add a club on approach, especially with wedges — smooth 46° from 110 rather
than a full 50°. On the range, note where the *pattern* centres, not the best
one.

**Target: short and long misses within 2× of each other in `TRENDS.md`.**

## How soon is a 6

Honestly: **12–18 months is the realistic answer. 6–9 months is the good case,
and it requires everything below to hold.**

The maths of the window matters. An index is the best 8 of the last 20, so even
if the level changed tomorrow you need ~20 rounds at the new standard before the
number reflects it. At your current ~9 rounds a month that is a two to three
month lag on top of whatever the improvement itself takes.

What the good case needs:
- The driver holds. **Only three rounds of post-lesson data exist**, and the
  hook has come back before. This is the single biggest unknown.
- Putts settle at 30 or under with the L.A.B.
- Doubles in your good rounds at one, not two.

What could push it past 18 months — and this is not a small risk:
- **Mexico City.** Unfamiliar courses have cost you 4–8 strokes every single
  time, and at ~7,350 feet every yardage in your bag changes. Expect the index
  to go *up* for a stretch after the move. Plan for it rather than being
  discouraged by it; re-learn your carry numbers early and treat the first ten
  rounds there as data collection.
- Layoffs. October 2025, April 2026 and July 2026 all follow breaks, and each
  cost more than any swing fault.

The thing worth saying: 26.7 to 9.2 in fourteen months is genuinely unusual.
But 9 to 6 is not the same kind of work as 22 to 9. The early gains came from
removing disasters; what is left is precision, and it arrives slower. Do not
read a flat month as failure.

## What not to do

Do not touch the swing. The driver lesson worked — GIR went 6 → 10 → 11 — and
your ball-striking is not what stands between you and a 6. Smooth tempo, and
leave it alone.
